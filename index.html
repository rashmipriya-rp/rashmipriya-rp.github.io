About

I’m a physicist studying how glasses and other amorphous solids deform, yield, and retain memories of prior deformation.

I completed my Ph.D. at Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Hyderabad, with Prof. Smarajit Karmakar. I used large-scale molecular dynamics to study how active particles change the strength and failure of ultrastable glasses, and how shear reversal reveals mechanical memory in active and passive glasses.

I also modeled and studied shear-induced yielding in rigid rods that form liquid-crystalline phases. I’m interested in how physics and AI can inform each other.

Featured Research

Active doping in ultrastable glasses

Nature Communications · 2026

In simulations, active particles could increase the yield stress and strain of ultrastable glasses and spread deformation across a network of shear bands, leading to more gradual yielding. I also ran stress-controlled simulations.

Paper Data and code Press coverage

Mechanical memory under shear reversal

Physical Review Materials · 2026 and Physical Review E · 2025

Shear reversal revealed an inverse Bauschinger effect in active ultrastable glasses. In passive glasses, preparation, shear rate, and deformation history determine when the response crosses over to the conventional Bauschinger effect.

Physical Review Materials Physical Review E

Methods. I use LAMMPS and custom MPI-parallel codes in C for large-scale molecular dynamics. I also work with Python.
