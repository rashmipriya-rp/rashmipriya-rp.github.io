Work

I study the mechanics of disordered matter — how glasses and amorphous solids deform, yield, and retain memories of their deformation history — using large-scale molecular dynamics simulations.

Much of my recent work explores what happens when disorder meets activity. In particular, I investigate how doping amorphous solids with self-propelled particles changes their yielding behaviour, strength, plastic deformation, and rheology. I am also interested in memory formation in driven, non-equilibrium systems.

I have also worked on liquid-crystalline phases and their mechanical response. Looking ahead, I am interested in learning and applying artificial intelligence and machine-learning methods at the interface of physics and complex systems.

Research

Active doping in amorphous solids
Active-particle doping as a control knob for the rigidity, yielding, and emergent rheology of dense disordered materials.
Yielding & memory
Directional memory in amorphous solids — the Bauschinger effect and its inverse — and the crossover between the two under steady shear.
Liquid crystals
Phase transitions and mechanical response in liquid-crystalline systems.
Large-scale simulation
Molecular dynamics with LAMMPS and custom MPI-parallel codes in C, run on high-performance computing clusters.
